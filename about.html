About Us

Our story starts with the land

Moving with Askiy was founded to close the physical and cultural distance between Indigenous people and the land that has always held them.

Our Mission

Walking together, back to the land

In the world of outdoor sports and activities, the land is the provider and caregiver of movement. Without the land, we would not be able to take part in enjoying the landscapes it offers.

We become balanced in all four directions when we enter into dialogue and interaction with the land. We pay our respects to all relations that help build this complex web of relations within Mother Earth.

To enjoy outdoor activities, we must be caregivers to the land as it has been given to us. The land has always had caregivers; First Peoples are and were the stewards of these territories that we all now play in.

However, there are systematic barriers that prohibit Indigenous peoples from accessing the outdoors and outdoor interaction. Within Treaty 6 and 8, there is a lack of Indigenous participation in outdoor activities, and Indigenous ways of doing and ways of being are disregarded and lost when moving on the land.

Moving with askiy was created to remove barriers and open the door to having more Indigenous peoples in the outdoors within Treaty 6 and 8. We want to foster connection and healing using sacred ecology and playing and living with traditional lands.

Leadership

The People Behind Moving with Askiy

Our team is grounded in relationship with these territories and committed to leading with community, not for it.

Sam Mockford (he/him)

Founding Director & Outreach

Sam Mockford is a proud dënesųłiné from Black Lake First Nation in Treaty 8 and of Scottish descent. Sam was born and raised in amiskwaciwâskahikan Treaty 6 (Edmonton). For generations, amiskwaciwâskahikan has been a gathering place for all nations to meet, connect, and trade. Many from Treaty 6 have taken Sam in and claim him as one of their own, and being from amiskwaciwâskahikan nêhiyaw ways of being, ways of doing, and lifeways have shaped Sam into seeing the askiy from an Indigenous perspective.

Growing up, Sam discovered snowboarding, which grew his love for the outdoors and the possibilities of being able to play and learn from the land at the same time. Sam worked in outdoor retail for 5 years at Breathe Outdoors, MEC, and Arc’teryx, which grew his love for hiking, backpacking, climbing, trail running, canoeing, and biking. This experience deepened his love for the land; however, as he moved through it, Sam saw little to no representation from Indigenous communities within the adventure sports. However when working for Arc’teryx Sam came across a film made by Arc’teryx called Iikaiksimatsa’pao’p: Grateful to Be on the Land about Cassie Ayoungman, founder of Soul of Miistaki. Soon after Sam was able to join Soul of Miistaki at a cultural climbing camp where he was inspired to do similar work in Treaty 6 and Treaty 8. Moving With askiy was born from an idea to help remove systemic barriers that prohibit Indigenous peoples in Treaty 6 and 8 from accessing the outdoors and outdoor interaction. Moving with the land is ceremony.

Alexia First Charger (they/them)

Administrative & Operations Coordinator

Alex First Charger is a queer/2S mixed Filipino from Olongapo City and Blackfoot from Kainai Blood Reserve in Treaty 7. Born and raised in Edmonton, they are entering their fourth year of Bachelor of Native Studies with a minor in Sociology, as well as an Indigenous Sports and Recreation certificate. Alex previously served as the Vice-President of Administration for the Indigenous Students' Union (ISU) during the 25/26 academic year and is currently a Sports and Recreation director for the ISU and works for miyomahcihowin as a Indigenous Wellness Student Mentor. Throughout this time, they have created multiple events such as Indigenous Student Climb Night and yoga and movement. Though Alex has been casually enjoying the outdoors all their life with hiking, camping, and backpacking, they recently got into climbing outdoors and got their first true taste of outdoor climbing through Soul of Miistaki's BIPOC Cultural Climbing Camp. Now with just over a year of experience, Alex has climbed all over the Rockies mountains, Northern Montana, and New York and assists with the Indigenous climbing club at uAlberta.

Culture and community are the driving forces behind their passion for creating safe spaces in outdoor recreation. Alex has experienced the immense impact that connecting land, ceremony, and community has done in their journey of reconnecting and is committed to make the outdoors more accessible to create more opportunities for Indigenous peoples.

Zoë Brygidyr (she/her)

Social Media Coordinator

Zoë is a proud member of the Métis Nation of Alberta and identifies as Métis Dënesųłıné from Fond Du Lac First Nation in Treaty 8 (maternal) and Ukrainian (paternal). She has lived near amiskwaciwâskahikan for over a decade and is grateful to have grown up learning from and playing on the lands of Treaty 6, 7, and 8 territories.

Zoë was fortunate to experience the outdoors from a young age, joining her family on backpacking and canoeing trips. Her love for the outdoors has continued to deepen over time, most recently through skiing, climbing, and her work as a river guide and interpreter. This allowed her to bring together her love for the outdoors and her cultural connection to the land, particularly kisiskâciwanisîpiy (North Saskatchewan River), which has been a source of life, gathering, connection, and activity for Indigenous communities since time immemorial. Currently, she is pursuing a bachelor’s degree in Native Studies and is eager to build a career dedicated to improving health and healing for Indigenous peoples.

Through these experiences, Zoë has become increasingly aware of how outdoor recreation and cultural connection are often separated, despite how deeply they can be intertwined for Indigenous peoples. Knowing that both can and do exist together, she is committed to helping create spaces for Indigenous youth like herself to learn, move, and grow in community and connection while rooted together in askiy.

Kowen Woo (he/him)

Web Developer & Outreach Coordinator

Kowen is of Métis and Chinese descent from Spruce Grove, Alberta. He is a descendant of the Desjarlais, Sayer, and Caplette families from the broader Red River settlement area in Treaty 1. Currently, he is pursuing a Bachelor’s degree in computer science with a focus on building community driven Artificial Intelligence systems for the revitalization of Indigenous culture and language.

Kowen’s journey into athletics began with competitive hockey, badminton, and basketball, but it was alpine skiing, hiking, and bouldering that sparked his deep connection to the outdoors and a desire to reconnect with the land through recreation. He is committed to sharing this passion with others, creating spaces where Indigenous peoples can thrive in outdoor activities. Recently, he has become fascinated with high wall climbing and looks forward to exploring multi-pitch and trad climbing in the near future.

Territorial Acknowledgment

Treaty 6 & Treaty 8 Territory

Moving with Askiy lives, works, and moves across Treaty 6 and Treaty 8 territory — the traditional and contemporary homelands of the Cree, Dene, Saulteaux, Blackfoot, Métis, and Nakota Sioux peoples. These treaties represent ongoing relationships and responsibilities, not a single historical moment.

We recognize First Peoples as the original stewards of this land, holders of knowledge that long predates our organization, and partners whose leadership guides how we do this work. We are committed to learning continually, building right relationship, and ensuring our programs honour the protocols of the nations whose territory we move across.
